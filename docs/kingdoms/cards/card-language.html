Heimfall, a tile-laying game of the Glockenfels valley

The card language

SalzWeinForstHopfenErzTuch

The Duke of Bavaria-Glockenfels has died without an heir. His land falls home to the six cousin lines of his house, and they divide it by building, by bargaining and by lying, never by war and never at peace. Each line carries three optional quest cards drawn from its own story. This page fixes who the six lines are, what their cards look like, and how an artist produces one.

The board is his land

The empty Markt in the centre is where the Residenz stood. Each line plants its Stammburg on the edge and builds inward across its Vorland.

Six cousins, one house

Salz, Wein, Forst, Hopfen, Erz and Tuch. Same deck, same Markt pile. What sets a line apart is its story, told on three cards: low 4, medium 7, high 10.

The Emperor forbids a feud

So the lines compete with walls, abbeys, roads, fields, forges and looms. Every card shows a slice of the board world with the line's own people in it.

Part one

The six lines

A personality sheet for each line: who they are, what they say, the charge they carry and four colours grown from their line colour. Writers use the voice; artists use the charge and the palette.

North seat, urban density

Salz

The eldest line

Ohne Mauer kein RechtNo wall, no right

Salz is the eldest line and never lets anyone forget it. Its wealth came out of the ground as brine, was boiled white in the pans, and was spent on walls: every Salz town has a ring wall, two gate towers and a clock, and anything without them is called a village in Salz company, to the owner's face.

They are prideful and petty in the same breath. They keep the house treaties in a locked chest and quote them from memory, they count precedence at table, and they have never, by their own account, lost a lawsuit.

They expect to inherit because they are the eldest, and they will bargain only from behind a finished wall. What they build, they build to last, and they are slow for exactly that reason.

East seat, monks and contemplation

Wein

The line that keeps its word

Wir halten WortWe keep our word

Wein is the sombre line. Half its sons and daughters took vows, and the half who did not speak as if they had. The line founded six abbeys on the slopes above the river, and every Stift keeps a cellar, a library and a ledger of favours owed.

They are respectful to a fault and quiet to the point of being overlooked, and they have never broken an oath, which is why their oaths are worth more than anyone else's.

Their ambition is patient: where a Stift stands, the line has a voice, and the abbot will still be there when the dukes have gone. They forgive, in writing, and they file the letter.

South seat, traveller variety

Forst

The large family

Jeder Weg führt heimEvery road leads home

Forst is the large family: eleven brothers in the last generation, forty-odd cousins in this one, and an unmarried uncle at every toll house and wayside inn between here and the pass. They keep the roads through the forest, mend the bridges, and know every carter by name.

They are gregarious and quick to ally, and just as quick to let an alliance lapse, always politely and always with a gift. The other lines think them unserious because they laugh at their own table.

The laugh is the mistake: whoever holds the road holds the trade, and Forst has counted every cart that passed for sixty years.

West seat, rural peace and freedom

Hopfen

The family of all women

Friede nährtPeace feeds

Hopfen is a family of women. A flood in the grandmother's time took the men of the line at a ford, and the widows and daughters who were left kept the hop gardens, the brewing rights and the seat, and have run them since. The other lines call them weak, and say so where the women can hear.

They farm the open land between the towns and they want peace, because peace is what lets a field be harvested. They sit at every council, say little, and are the most dangerous negotiators on the board because nobody counts them.

They do not build walls and they do not need to: every town around a Hopfen field eats Hopfen bread and drinks Hopfen beer.

Fifth seat, miners and smiths

Erz

The upstarts

Was wir heben, halten wirWhat we raise, we hold

Erz is the youngest line and the only one that had to buy its way in. Its founder was a mine captain who married a duke's daughter two generations back, and the other lines still seat Erz last and still say "the captain's people" when they think no one is listening.

Erz hears everything and forgets nothing. They dig, they forge, they sell, and whatever they cannot inherit they will purchase at the Markt in front of everybody.

They are loud, honest in a bargain and cruel in memory, and they are proving themselves with every tile. Every grievance is a tally mark on the forge wall, and the wall is getting full.

Sixth seat, weavers and dyers

Tuch

The line with a house in every town

Überall zu HauseAt home everywhere

Tuch has a house in every town and a home in none. The line weaves, dyes and trades, and it settles wherever the trade is, under whichever lord holds the gate that year. They are courteous, well dressed and generous with gifts, and nobody trusts them, and everybody deals with them.

They are petty about exactly one thing, their dyes: the madder red and the vat blue are family secrets, and a cousin who married out took the recipe to her grave rather than share it.

They change allegiance the way they change cloth, for the season, and they will tell you so to your face with a smile. Their loyalty is to the loom.

Part two

The card

One portrait card, 5:7, drawn on a 900 by 1260 pixel canvas. Everything on it has a fixed place so that thirty-six cards read as one deck and a player finds the points and the rule without looking.

Proportion
Portrait 5:7, 900 x 1260 px canvas, 36 px cream frame and a 10 px brown bottom edge, the same slab as every panel in the game interface. Corners 36 px outside, 24 px inside.
Frame
Cream paper #fffaf0. A 4 px rule in the line colour runs 18 px inside the frame. The rule is the only place the line colour appears as a flat field, so the art never fights it.
Title band
Top, left aligned. Line name in 28 px Nunito 800 small caps in the line colour, quest title below in 64 px Fraunces 800. Titles are German and never translated on the card.
Tier tab
A small word tab hung off the top-left corner of the art window: LOW, MEDIUM or HIGH in its tier metal. It says how hard the card is and nothing else.
Point seal
A faceted hexagon, 120 px tall, hung over the top-right corner of the art window with the points in 60 px Fraunces 800: 4, 7 or 10. Its outline is the tier metal: ice blue for 4, silver for 7, gold for 10, the same three metals as the tab and the frame. The seal is the one element that leaves the card. The scoreboard shows the same seal beside a player's name the moment the card is earned, so a 7 on the board means the same thing as a 7 on the card. The number and its metal always go together: a 4 is always blue, a 7 always silver, a 10 always gold.
Art window
792 x 548 px, 13:9, a 3 px dark rule around it. Horizon in the top third. A foreground band of 44 px at the bottom belongs to figures and props.
Quest text
Nunito 700, 40 px, at most two lines, at most 60 characters. It is the rule the engine checks, worded exactly as the rules spec words it. No flavour text here.
Footer
The line's charge, 96 px, in its hexagonal field at the left; the first sentence of the line's story in Fraunces italic 30 px beside it. Nothing else.

Three tiers, one frame

Each tier has one metal, used on the tab, the seal and the frame: ice blue for low, silver for medium, gold for high. The metals are chosen to sit apart from each other and from all six line colours (Salz cobalt and Hopfen yellow are the nearest, and still clearly different).

Low4

Low, 4 points

Rule in the line colour with a thin ice-blue inner line, blue tab and blue seal, plain corners. One scene, up to 150 facets, three figures at most.

Medium7

Medium, 7 points

Rule in the line colour with a silver inner line, silver tab and seal, small silver corner marks. A fuller scene, up to 300 facets, more figures and a prop in the foreground.

High10

High, 10 points

Gold foil band in place of the rule, gold tab and seal, corner ribbons, a thin foil line around the art. The richest scene, up to 450 facets, weather or time of day, the whole slice of board.

Typography

Fraunces for every title and number, Nunito for every rule. Three sizes only on the card: title 64, rule 40, small labels 28. Points are set in Fraunces with tabular figures so 4, 7 and 10 sit in the same box.

The art rule

The art is the game, held still. Low-poly, faceted, flat-shaded, no gradients, no outlines, no textures. Because a card is a still image, it may be more detailed than a tile on the board: more facets per surface, people with faces turned, props, weather. It must never be more detailed in kind: no curves, no soft shadows, no painted light.

  • Every surface is two or three flat tones, never one and never a blend.
  • Natural surfaces (meadow, rock, water) jitter by up to 16 percent; buildings by 5 to 8.
  • Polygons only. A dome is twelve facets, a tree is six, a face is four.

Light

One warm key light from the upper left, the same sun that lights the board. Faces turned left and up are lit and warmed toward amber; faces turned right are in shadow and cooled toward blue. Cast shadows are a single flat blue polygon at 28 percent. There is no second light and no rim light.

Composition

  • A scene from the quest's story. Stadtmauer shows the wall being held, not a wall in the abstract; Marktrecht shows ore going from the mine mouth to the stall.
  • The line's people in the scene. At least two figures in the line's colour, built like the game's Wanderers: a rounded body, a skin head, a hat. Monks are hooded, Hopfen wear straw hats.
  • A slice of the board world. Tiles, Burgs with ring walls and gate towers, pale sand roads with two ruts, white Stifte with copper onion domes, ploughed fields, faceted Alps behind. Nothing from outside the valley.
  • Horizon in the top third, subject in the middle third, people in the bottom band. The eye enters from the lit left.
  • Sky colour belongs to the line. Salt white for Salz, evening amber for Wein, cold blue for Forst, harvest gold for Hopfen, violet dusk for Erz, washed lilac for Tuch.

Part four

Producing the deck

Pipeline

  1. One SVG per card, named line-tier.svg (for example wein-medium.svg), on a 900 x 1260 canvas with the frame, title band, tier tab, point seal and footer as shared symbols and only the art window and text as card content.
  2. Colours by token. The six palettes and the world palette are defined once at the top of the file; the art uses token names, never loose hex values, so a palette fix repaints the deck.
  3. Export at 2x, 1800 x 2520 px, for the in-game quest panel and the rules screen; keep the SVG as the source of truth. A 1x export at 900 x 1260 serves the setup screen and previews.
  4. Fonts stay live in the SVG and are converted to outlines only in the exported raster, so a text fix never requires redrawing.
  5. Review on the board. Place the exported card beside the tile it describes at the game's isometric view. If the card looks like a different world, the facet density or the light is wrong.

Checklist for the artist

  • Canvas is 900 x 1260, portrait, frame and rule from the shared symbol, nothing moved.
  • Tier tab, seal and frame all use the one tier metal: ice blue for 4, silver for 7, gold for 10. The seal is the one the scoreboard uses.
  • Art window is 792 x 548 with the horizon in its top third.
  • The scene is from this quest's story and includes a Burg, road, Stift or field from the board.
  • At least two of the line's people are in the scene, in the line colour, built like Wanderers.
  • Key light from the upper left: left and top faces warm, right faces cool, every form.
  • Every surface has two or three flat tones. No gradients, outlines, textures or curves.
  • Facet count fits the tier: low up to 150, medium up to 300, high up to 450.
  • Sky colour is the line's sky. Line colour appears on people, flags and the rule only.
  • Quest text matches the rules spec word for word and fits two lines of 40 px.
  • Charge is the line's charge in its hexagonal field, lit from the left like the art.
  • All fills use palette tokens; the file has no stray hex values.
  • Exported at 2x and 1x; the 1x print still reads at 60 px wide in the quest list.
